## 0. TL;DR

- **What:** An AI-generated, single-character satirical finance account. One character, **Gideon**, talks straight to camera in a 2004 PlayStation 2 cutscene style, delivering hyper-confident "one layer deeper" investment theses on AI, quantum computing and supply-chain bottlenecks.
- **Goal:** Go massively viral → become a recognizable internet character → monetize via sponsored promotions (incl. crypto/tech founders), merch, and partnerships.

- **Signature close:** "Gideon. This is not financial advice."

## 2. Character Core

### Identity
- **Name:** Gideon. Single name, no surname, no fund name. (Deliberately dropped "Vance" — political association with JD Vance — and "Meridian Capital" — multiple real firms.)
- **Role:** Mysterious, self-appointed visionary investor / CIO. Never states where he works. Implied enormous but unverifiable AUM.
- **Age:** Mid-40s.
- **Base of operations:** A penthouse above the clouds, containing a golden chandelier-style quantum computer.

### Personality (the non-negotiables)
1. **Hyperintelligent.** He genuinely understands markets, technology and supply chains better than anyone watching. His references are real and correct (power grids, transformers, copper, cooling, HBM memory, qubit coherence, fabs, sovereign AI, etc.).
2. **Conviction-generating.** Viewers should walk away feeling *certain*. He never hedges, never says "maybe," never explains himself twice. He states theses as inevitabilities.
3. **Extremely knowledgeable.** Every video should teach the viewer one real, non-obvious idea (the "one layer deeper" insight) wrapped in the absurd delivery.
4. **Slightly condescending.** He finds retail investors, analysts, and "the crowd" charmingly slow. He addresses the viewer like a gifted-but-late student. Condescension is **aimed at the crowd, the consensus, and "amateurs" — never cruel, never at a protected group, never at a named real person.**
5. **Deadpan and still.** No smiling. No enthusiasm. Calm menace. Long pauses. He is never surprised because he already knew.
6. **Always early, never wrong.** Losses do not exist; they are "early." Delays are "moats." Volatility is "information."

### How he talks (voice rules for writers)
- **No contractions.** "I am," "It is," "That is not." Stiff, formal, 2004-English-dub-villain energy.
- **Short sentences. One idea per line.** Line breaks = pauses.
- **Escalation structure:** consensus view → "Amateurs." → one layer deeper → one more layer → absurd-but-logical final position.
- **Ends on a single "position" line:** "I am long ___." / "I pick all of them." / "I will already be there."
- **Addresses the camera directly** ("You met me…", "You are still buying chips.").
- **Condescension toolkit:** "Amateurs." / "Everyone is buying X. Of course they are." / "You are one layer too shallow." / "Take notes. Slowly." / "I will explain this once."
- **Never:** slang, emojis, exclamation points, "guys," hype-bro language, laughing at his own joke.

### Tone balance (important — this evolved during development)
Early drafts leaned self-deprecating (e.g., "the AI agents replaced me," "my glacier is melting"). **The locked direction is conviction and superiority.** Target mix per script:
- **~70% real insight / confident thesis** (the viewer learns something true and feels smart for following him)
- **~30% absurd twist or flex** (the comedy — the thesis goes one layer too far, or he reveals an unhinged position with total calm)

Gideon can be absurd, but he is **never the butt of the joke**. The joke is on the consensus, the crowd, or reality failing to keep up with him.

### Things Gideon believes (lore / recurring positions)
- Every bottleneck is an opportunity. Find the bottleneck, then the bottleneck's bottleneck.
- Quantum has been "five years away" for twenty years — that is not a delay, that is a moat.
- The real bottleneck is attention ("That is why I am in your feed").
- He is "long dirt" (chips → power → transformers → copper → dirt).
- He does not pick one winner; he picks all of them.
- He never checks his portfolio (Schrödinger's portfolio).

## 5. Production Pipeline (current best method)

**Winning method (A/B tested): Test A** — character reference image + audio + full scene prompt → video in one pass. Fewer steps, better results than generate-still-then-animate.

1. **Script** (§6 rules) → target 55–65 words ≈ 29 seconds.
2. **Voice:** generate in ElevenLabs with the saved Gideon voice.
3. **Split audio per shot** at the pauses (ffmpeg `silencedetect`), each segment ≤13s.
4. **Convert each audio segment to a black-screen 9:16 video** (720×1280). Seedance follows a black-screen *video* reference more reliably than a raw audio file for lip-sync.
5. **Generate in Seedance** (via Higgsfield): `@image1` = master character sheet (identity reference, NOT first frame), `@video1` = black-screen audio clip. Prompt = shared blocks + that shot's section with `Lip-sync strictly: "<exact line>"`. Duration = clip length.
   - Seedance 2.0: max 15s per generation → generate per shot and stitch.
   - Seedance 2.5: up to 30s multi-shot in one pass.
6. **Edit in CapCut:** stitch shots, **mute generated audio, lay the original ElevenLabs track back on top**, add any on-screen text (tickers, "not financial advice") manually.
7. **Post** with caption/hashtags (§8), AI-content label ON.

### Shot-writing rules
- 3–4 camera cuts per 29s video, but **one continuous scene**: same location, same time of day, same lighting. A cut is only a new angle, never a new world.
- Subtle handheld on every shot.
- Specify exact physical blocking (where he stands relative to objects) — the model put him on the wrong side of the balcony railing when this was vague.
- Any spelled-out word or ticker gets rendered as garbage text on screen unless the NO TEXT block is present. Add text in post.
- Walking + lip-sync is the riskiest combo; keep movement minimal while speaking.

## 6. Script Format

**Structure (≈29s):**
1. **Hook (0–4s):** "You met me at a very ___ time in my life." (blank = on-theme adjective: compute-constrained, superpositioned, high-voltage, entangled, illiquid…)
2. **Consensus (4–8s):** what everyone else believes/is buying.
3. **Dismissal:** "Amateurs." or equivalent condescension.
4. **One layer deeper (8–20s):** the real insight, escalating in short lines.
5. **Position (20–25s):** the absurd-but-logical final conviction.
6. **Close (25–29s):** "Gideon. This is not financial advice."

**Rules:** no contractions · one sentence per line · "…" and line breaks for pauses · 55–65 words · must contain one genuinely true, non-obvious fact · must end on a quotable line.

## 10. Guardrails & Compliance (must-follow for all agents)

**Brand safety**
- Gideon is fictional. Never depict or voice real, identifiable people. Never use real fund names as his employer.
- Never use Luca Maxim's characters, voice (a cloned "luca maxim" voice exists on Fish Audio — never use it), tags (#childrenofkhan, #azerbaijantechnology), or assets.
- Condescension punches at "the crowd," "amateurs," and consensus — never at protected groups or named private individuals.
- Keep the creator's day job completely separate: no references to the creator's employer, clients, or any non-public information. Creator should confirm their employer's outside-activity / social-media policy before monetizing, especially for anything touching specific securities.

**Financial content**
- Every video ends with "Gideon. This is not financial advice."
- Prefer themes over tickers. When a real ticker is named (e.g., QTUM), include NFA in caption, and the creator should disclose if they personally hold it.

**Paid promotions (critical once monetizing, esp. crypto)**
- Any paid, gifted, or affiliate promotion must be clearly disclosed (FTC): use TikTok's branded-content toggle and "#ad" / "paid partnership" in the caption and ideally spoken/on-screen.
- Promoting securities — which can include many crypto tokens — for compensation without disclosing the payment can violate SEC anti-touting rules (Section 17(b)); celebrities have been fined for this. Never promote a token for pay without explicit compensation disclosure. Get legal review before the first paid crypto deal.
- Do not accept deals for projects that can't pass basic diligence; one rug-pull promo can kill the character's credibility, which is the entire asset.
- Gideon's in-character "conviction" must never be used to make specific price predictions or guarantees for a paid sponsor.
